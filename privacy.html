The Livonne Privacy Promise

A system that understands your life should still belong to you.

Livonne uses ambient sensing to provide useful help without waiting for someone to ask the perfect question. That only works if the person—not Livonne, a care organization, or even their family—controls what becomes memory and who can access it.

The Livonne Privacy Promise has three parts.

01

We keep only what helps.

Audio, video, motion, and other household signals are working material—not a permanent record of your life.

Livonne processes those signals locally. Most disappear without ever becoming stored information. When something is worth remembering, Livonne distills the raw signal into a useful memory: the morning routine was completed, a reminder worked, a preference changed, or something needs attention.

The useful understanding remains. The unnecessary footage, audio, and sensor history disappear.

There are narrow exceptions. If a serious fall, break-in, or other authorized event occurs, Livonne may preserve the relevant moments when that information could help the person or someone responding. It keeps the event—not the rest of the day.

02

You own what remains.

Livonne memory belongs to the person or household that created it in a meaningful, practical way.

Retained memory stays within the household system. It is not transmitted elsewhere except for encrypted backups controlled by the owner. Livonne employees, family members, care providers, and outside services do not receive access unless the owner chooses to disclose something to them.

Ownership means you can:

  • see what Livonne remembers;
  • correct something it misunderstood;
  • decide who may receive specific information;
  • revoke that permission;
  • move your continuity to another Livonne system;
  • delete individual memories;
  • destroy the entire record, including its backups.

Sharing is limited by purpose. A kitchen may need to know about a dietary restriction. It does not need the person’s medical history. A maintenance worker may need the repair request and access instructions. They do not need the household’s routines or family relationships.

No one gets the whole person simply because they have a role in one task.

03

The outside world gets only what it needs.

Livonne can also protect the person when they leave the privacy of their household system and interact with the open internet.

When a real website needs personal information to complete a transaction, Livonne shows what the site is requesting and asks the user to authorize the disclosure. Information is not silently handed over because a form asked for it.

When a site has no legitimate reason to know someone’s real identity, the user can choose an alias instead. Livonne can remember which alias was used with each service, keeping those interactions separate from the person’s primary identity.

That creates a useful defense. If a salesperson or scammer later calls using an alias known only to one service, the user immediately knows where the information came from—and that the caller does not know their real identity.

Livonne does not falsify material information when identity, safety, eligibility, pricing, legal obligations, or the integrity of a transaction depends on the truth.

The system knows the real person.

The outside world receives only what the person authorizes and the task actually requires.
