Livonne Legacy

Livonne Legacy helps families stay connected while life is happening—and preserve what matters before it disappears. It carries moments across distance, lets useful family context move between homes, and gives photographs and conversations the stories that make them meaningful.

Nothing is inherited by default. People choose what is saved, what is shared, and what is passed forward. Livonne preserves the person’s own words and selected memories—not an artificial version designed to speak in their place.

Key capabilities

Presence

Family Presence

Private, permissioned connection between the people and homes that make up a family. Photographs, messages, calls, celebrations, and ordinary moments can reach the people who care while they are still fresh.

Continuity

Continuity Between Homes

Useful context can travel with the person who needs it. A baby’s current routines can follow them to Grandma’s house. Familiar bedtime music, recent changes, and the little things that work remain available without a frantic handoff at the door.

Memory

Living Memory

When a photograph, place, or conversation brings a story back, Livonne can help preserve it in the person’s own voice—with their permission. The pauses, laughter, and small details remain part of the memory.

Choice

Chosen Legacy

People decide what they want to leave behind. Stories, advice, and selected memories can remain with the family, while private personal context is deleted. Livonne preserves what the person chose to give; it does not create an imitation to speak in their place.

The Legacy story

Legacy

A family game night becomes the moment everyone learns that a new generation is on the way.

From pregnancy through sleepless nights, Livonne helps new parents carry the details their exhausted memories cannot. When their son spends his first evening at Grandma’s house, his routines travel with him—along with the small changes, preferences, and familiar comforts that make another home feel safe.

Years later, that child is grown and riding down the coast when Livonne finds a story connected to the road beneath him.

The voice belongs to his great-grandfather, Ron.

Jacob never met him.

But he knows that laugh.
