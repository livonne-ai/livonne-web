Connor came through the door, dropped his backpack, and reached for the game controller.

As he passed the Surface, his orc king appeared.

“You have a history test tomorrow.”

Connor stopped.

“…oh.”

“The test covers the fall of the Roman Empire.”

“How bad is it?”

“You missed eight questions on the class review.”

“So pretty bad.”

“I can give you a quick overview, walk through the review, or load the strategy game your class has been using.”

Connor looked at the controller.

“What’s the game one?”

The Surface changed.

A map of Europe appeared, divided into territories. Armies moved along the borders. Trade routes crossed the Mediterranean.

Connor set down the controller.

“Fine. Show me why Rome is falling apart.”

“Where would you like to begin?”

“Barbarians.”

“Popular choice.”

Upstairs, Sarah was halfway through her math homework when she stopped at a problem that made no sense.

She opened her Livonne copilot on the computer.

“Can you help me with this?”

“You changed the sign on the second line.”

Sarah looked at her work.

“I know. That’s what the example did.”

“The example changed the sign because it moved the term to the other side of the equation. You haven’t done that yet.”

Sarah stared at it.

“I still don’t get it.”

“This is the same kind of problem you were working on last week. Remember how we separated the variables first?”

The earlier problem appeared beside the one she was working on.

Sarah looked between them.

“Oh.”

She erased the second line and tried again.

“Like that?”

“Exactly.”

Sarah finished the next step on her own.

Then the next.

When she reached the answer, Livonne gave her another problem using the same pattern.

This time, she did not need help.

Downstairs, Connor finished the strategy game and picked up his regular controller.

His headset connected.

“Let’s run through the review while you play,” Livonne said.

Connor started the game.

“Fine.”

The questions came between matches.

Some he answered immediately. A few took longer.

He missed two.

The next morning, Maya dropped him off outside the school.

Connor opened the door, then felt his phone buzz.

“Two questions before you head in,” Livonne said.

Connor stood beside the car with his backpack over one shoulder.

“Come on.”

“You asked me to make sure you remembered them.”

“Fine.”

Livonne gave him the first question.

He answered correctly.

Then the second.

He hesitated, thought about it, and got that one too.

“Good luck.”

Connor slipped the phone into his backpack before walking into school.

“I don’t need luck.”

“No,” Livonne said. “But you did need those two questions.”

Connor closed the car door before the orc could say anything else.

By Saturday, Maya had a different problem.

The dishwasher was full.

The trash was still in the kitchen.

A basket of clean laundry had made it upstairs but stopped in the hallway, six feet from the bedrooms where it belonged.

Maya walked up to the Surface.

It woke as she approached.

“Hey, Livonne. We are having a hell of a time managing chores as a family. Do you have anything for that?”

“There’s an app called Chore Bounty.”

“What does it do?”

“It turns the kids’ weekly allowance into a live chore board. Each job has a cash value. Jobs nobody wants gradually pay more. Popular jobs pay less. The app tracks completion and settles the allowance at the end of the week.”

Maya looked toward the laundry basket.

“Show me.”

A few minutes later, Sarah and Connor stood in front of the board.

Trash: two dollars.

Dishwasher: three dollars.

Laundry: four dollars.

Bathroom: six dollars.

Connor pointed at the bathroom.

“Wait. I get six dollars for that?”

“If you claim it and finish it,” Livonne said.

Sarah stepped closer.

“So if I take the dishwasher and the laundry, I get seven?”

“Correct.”

“I’ll do that.”

Connor looked at her.

“You took both of those.”

“You were staring at the bathroom.”

“Because nobody wants the bathroom.”

“That’s why it pays six dollars.”

Connor looked back at the board.

The bathroom was still waiting.

He claimed it.

A moment later, Sarah tried to take out the trash too.

The value dropped.

“What happened?” she asked.

“You already claimed two jobs,” Livonne said. “Chore Bounty lowers the value of quick, popular work when one person begins taking all of it.”

Connor smiled.

“I like this app.”

“You haven’t cleaned the bathroom yet,” Sarah said.

“I like the theory of this app.”

By Sunday afternoon, the dishwasher was empty.

The laundry had reached the bedrooms.

The bathroom was clean.

The trash had eventually become valuable enough for Connor to take it out without being asked.

The kids still complained. Of course they did.

But now they complained about the board.

Not about whether Mom was being unfair.

And Maya was no longer assigning the work, tracking it, or calculating what everyone was owed.

Chore Bounty handled that.

The family simply used it.
