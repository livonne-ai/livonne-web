A Different Decision

Ron and Maya at Home

Download full story

For a while, the network is enough.

Meals arrive twice a week. The lawn is handled. Physical therapy happens every Tuesday. Livonne keeps the routines coordinated, notices when something slips, and brings in help before Maya has to go looking for it.

Then Ron begins needing a little more assistance around the house.

Livonne finds a local home aide who already supports several families in Ron’s neighborhood and two nearby communities. She has verified training, strong recommendations from families in the network, and room in her schedule.

At first, she visits twice a week.

She helps Ron prepare a few meals, handles some light household tasks, and makes sure he is ready for appointments.

Eventually, twice a week becomes every weekday.

Then some weekends too.

The arrangement is working. Ron likes her. She knows the house, understands his routines, and has permission to see only the information she needs while she is there.

But gradually, the visits begin changing.

One afternoon, Maya arrives as the aide is leaving.

“What did she help with today?” Maya asks.

Ron shrugs.

“Nothing much.”

“Nothing?”

“We had coffee. Talked about the game.”

Maya looks at the two cups on the table.

“You scheduled a home aide so you would have someone to watch football with?”

“I didn’t schedule her. Livonne did.”

Livonne answers from the kitchen screen.

“Ron requested an additional visit.”

Ron looks betrayed.

“You’re supposed to be on my side.”

“I answered the question accurately.”

Maya sits down across from him.

“Dad, it seems like you’re calling the aide over because you want someone to talk to.”

“I like talking to her.”

“I know. But that’s a very expensive way to have a friendship.”

Ron looks down at his coffee.

The practical needs are covered.

That is not the problem anymore.

The problem is that Ron spends most of his days alone.

Livonne can keep him oriented. It can help him manage the house, connect him with family, and bring services to the door.

It cannot put another person at the kitchen table unless someone is being paid to sit there.

Maya moves her chair closer.

“Livonne has started something new. They’re building Livonne Communities.”

Ron gives her a suspicious look.

“A nursing home.”

“A retirement community.”

“That sounds like something a nursing home would call itself.”

“It isn’t a hospital, Dad. You would have your own apartment. Your own things. Your own kitchen.”

“Then what’s the point?”

“You could cook when you want to. Or eat in the shared dining room when you don’t. There would be nurses and support staff on campus, so we wouldn’t have to schedule a separate visit every time you needed a little help.”

Ron listens but does not look convinced.

“They also share services,” Maya continues. “Transportation, maintenance, meals, activities. You would still choose what you use.”

“And what happens to all of this?”

He gestures toward the kitchen screen and the devices around the house.

“You take Livonne with you.”

“The same one?”

“The same one. Your memories, your routines, your permissions—everything. You wouldn’t have to teach a new system who you are.”

Ron looks toward the screen.

“Is that true?”

“Yes,” Livonne says. “Your continuity belongs to you. If you move, it moves with you.”

The room becomes quiet again.

Then Maya says the part that matters most.

“And there would be people your age there, Dad. People who remember the same things you do. People who want someone to watch the game with them too.”

Ron looks back at the second coffee cup.

“I’m not giving up my independence.”

“I’m not asking you to.”

“It sounds like you are.”

“I’m asking whether living alone is still the best use of it.”

Ron does not answer immediately.

Maya lets the question sit.

Finally, he looks at her.

“Can we see the place before everyone starts packing my house?”

“No one is packing anything.”

“I know how these things work.”

“We’ll take a tour. That’s all.”

Ron nods.

“One tour.”

“One tour.”

For years, the goal had been to keep Ron in his home.

Livonne helped them do that far longer than Maya once thought possible.

But staying independent and staying in the same building are not always the same thing.

Sometimes independence means having enough support to keep living on your own terms.

And sometimes it means having someone nearby who wants to stay for the game.
